Runtime Jobs

Magpie starts several long-running routines on backend startup.

For the guided explanation of coordination and replicas, see background jobs. This reference records the routine families, intervals, and limits.

How the jobs fit together​

FamilyPurposeWhere to understand it
ProducersDiscover routes, check them, collect enrichment, and record traffic usage.Import, checker, rotation.
Consumers and derived viewsCommit check batches, update usage, refresh reputation, source counts, and dashboards.Results and storage.
Evaluation and deliveryRead existing measurements, track incidents, and send durable outbox messages.Alerts.
Maintenance and recoveryRetain bounded history, reclaim work, expire old records, and rebuild lost queues.Startup and recovery.

Shutdown stops producers first, then consumers, then read-model refreshes. Coordination differs by routine: queue leases, stream consumer groups, Redis leadership, and PostgreSQL delivery claims solve different problems.

Statistics consumers flush at 5,000 physical results or every 15 seconds. They bound transactions by 5,000 potential latest-evidence rows, keeping a single result atomic even if it exceeds that limit. All transactions and preparation in a flush share a 30-second deadline. Committed prefixes survive later failures; only handled original stream IDs can be acknowledged. Memory workers retry unfinished final buffers until empty or the overall shutdown fails.

Proxy-list read models coalesce dirty route IDs, discover their owners, and refresh at most 5,000 workspace/route rows per prefix by default. Each prefix has a 30-second deadline. Metadata reads use only its candidate routes. A normal pass handles at most four projection statements on its five-second timer. It retains unfinished ownership pairs for retry without repeating committed prefixes. When a projection prefix reaches its child deadline while its parent refresh context remains active, the next invocation halves its width within that failed interval. Successful smaller statements commit independently; completing the interval restores ordinary width. Discovery, source-lookup, parent cancellation, and permanent-error paths do not trigger splitting. Smaller retries can add database round trips and still need representative throughput validation. Source invalidations follow all completed owner prefixes for the original route batch; a failed source lookup retries separately. Final draining retries proxy and source failures with a 250 ms backoff until both dirty queues are empty. Source-counter batches have their own 30-second deadline. Unfinished work makes the overall shutdown deadline report an error. The environment reference lists the READ_MODEL_REFRESH_* controls.

Read-model failure logs distinguish numeric stages: 1 is owner discovery, 2 is a chosen projection prefix, and 3 is its final source lookup. cursor, attempted_pairs, owner_pairs, retry_end, and retry_pair_limit describe in-memory progress without logging route arrays or credentials. A prefix stage includes its existing table check; it does not prove the upsert reached the server. Timing fields report milliseconds for owner, projection and source work. These fields support diagnosis and do not certify capacity.

Dashboard refreshes pass job cancellation into all database reads, including delayed daily-counter backfills. Every 30 seconds, the normal timer snapshots components requested within the last two minutes and refreshes them serially, rechecking demand before each component. It does not scan every workspace or renew demand itself. Info, recent checks and fastest routes are independent keys, including custom list limits. Demand and cached entries are process-local and expire after idle; restarting clears them.

Each successful entry is eligible for cache hits for at most 30 seconds. A cold or expired request loads synchronously or joins the same component's refresh. Each loader inherits its caller context and a 30-second child bound. Canceled or failed refreshes retain the previous stored snapshot without granting an expired hit. Delayed daily-counter backfills follow the refresh's cancellation and completion lifecycle. Historical counter backfill for unread workspaces waits until their dashboard counts are requested; this timer is not an unattended all-workspace historical-backfill job. The explicit force prewarm still visits all workspaces for strict development fixture startup. Judge and fastest-route queries retain their existing workspace scope and query fan-out; fastest-route metadata loads after selecting the winners.

Leadership and coordination​

Some routines are executed with leader-election semantics using Redis locks to avoid duplicate execution across instances.

Leadership, configuration subscriptions, checker settings, and stream startup retry when Redis returns. Lost consumer groups restart with database replay protection. Queue marker checks run every ten seconds; missing queues rebuild from PostgreSQL under shared leadership, with readiness at 503 after detection.

Statistics maintenance precreates months even when migrations or history retention are disabled, moves affected DEFAULT rows, and compacts older daily counts into lifetime totals. Stream maintenance retires acknowledged identities behind a durable replay floor. Orphan cleanup drains counter tables hourly with a 45-second budget and retries failures or interruptions after one minute by default.

Obsolete latest-evidence cleanup evaluates at most PROXY_STATISTICS_RETENTION_BATCH_SIZE candidate pointers per page in primary-key order. It runs once per coordinated history-worker round. An unfinished scan resumes on the next scheduled pass, within the existing batch and runtime limits. Each scan fixes its upper key before starting, then returns to earlier keys in the next scan so new evidence cannot indefinitely postpone older invalidations. Dirty workspace namespaces and current legacy, default, and tag-override evidence remain protected; history retention keeps their referenced checks.

Monitor magpie_leader_job_active, magpie_maintenance_last_success_timestamp_seconds, magpie_maintenance_failures_total, and magpie_statistics_replay_floor_timestamp_seconds. An old pending event delays ledger retirement.

Minute sweeps expire DNS/robots caches, bounded to 20,000/2,000 entries. Public-IP lookup repeats every 15 minutes after success. Rotators close client/upstream sockets when stopped and reload changed valid TLS certificates on handshake.

The durable email outbox is split across two coordination models:

  • Every backend instance runs the email delivery worker and can poll, claim, and send queued outbox rows in parallel.
  • Outbox housekeeping remains leader-coordinated to avoid duplicate stale-message recovery and terminal-row cleanup work. Both sent and abandoned messages expire.

Timers​

Most intervals are configured in global settings (checker_timer, scraper_timer, judge_timer, blacklist_timer, runtime timers, GeoLite update timer).

Retention routine interval/limits are configured via environment variables:

  • PROXY_STATISTICS_RETENTION_INTERVAL / PROXY_STATISTICS_RETENTION_INTERVAL_MINUTES
  • PROXY_STATISTICS_RETENTION_DAYS
  • PROXY_STATISTICS_RESPONSE_RETENTION_DAYS
  • PROXY_STATISTICS_RETENTION_BATCH_SIZE
  • PROXY_STATISTICS_RETENTION_MAX_BATCHES

Password recovery maintenance is configured via:

  • PASSWORD_RESET_CLEANUP_INTERVAL / PASSWORD_RESET_CLEANUP_INTERVAL_MINUTES
  • EMAIL_OUTBOX_POLL_INTERVAL / EMAIL_OUTBOX_POLL_INTERVAL_SECONDS
  • EMAIL_OUTBOX_BATCH_SIZE
  • EMAIL_PROCESSING_TIMEOUT / EMAIL_PROCESSING_TIMEOUT_SECONDS
  • EMAIL_OUTBOX_RETENTION_HOURS
  • EMAIL_RETRY_BASE_SECONDS
  • EMAIL_MAX_ATTEMPTS

The workspace invitation cleanup routine runs hourly under a Redis leader lock and deletes expired invitation rows. New invitation lifetime is configured with WORKSPACE_INVITATION_TTL_HOURS; cleanup does not renew or archive invitations.

Alert evaluation runs every minute under its own Redis leader lock and reads existing measurements only for enabled rules. Each pass has a 45-second deadline and four PostgreSQL workers. Each worker processes a workspace's enabled rules together, starting with the least recently attempted workspace. One persisted attempt marker advances the workspace's priority before measurement; successful state writes stamp all observed rules in the same batch. A timeout or leadership change leaves unattempted workspaces first in the next pass. Attempts do not count as incident observations.

The shared rolling-history scan has a 15-second budget. Whole-workspace route counts share grouped reads with a five-second budget. Each distinct pool-count measurement has five seconds, with at most ten seconds for all pool measurements in a workspace. Scheduling and state writes each have a fresh three-second budget. Identical pool filters share counts within the workspace batch. Measurement failures produce Unknown without consuming the whole pass. Rule state, bulk incident transitions, and bounded delivery-record inserts commit together under database locks, with rule-revision and checker-generation checks. History cleanup has its own ten-second budget. Closed incidents and their deliveries expire after 90 days. Orphan route cleanup retains the last 15 minutes of check history so deleting the final managed association cannot erase recent failures.

The opt-in backend test TestAlertsScaleCadencePostgres validates six consecutive passes for 2,000 workspaces with 100 rules each. On two-CPU PostgreSQL 17 with GOMAXPROCS=2, all 200,000 rules completed each pass in 19 to 30 seconds with one destination per rule. This includes opening and recovering every incident and queuing 400,000 notification records. The backend alert ADR records the exact workload and command. Projection size, history volume, pool filters, notification fanout, and other database traffic determine capacity for a deployment.

Each instance runs ten alert delivery workers. The dispatcher claims only free slots and refills them as sends finish, draining eligible messages continuously. It polls every five seconds when no messages are eligible or a claim fails. PostgreSQL SKIP LOCKED claims distribute work, and claim tokens fence stale workers. Processing claims become eligible again after five minutes. HTTP requests have a 15-second timeout; SMTP conversations have a 30-second total timeout and support cancellation. Messages retry up to four times. Alert evaluation and delivery add no operations to the proxy checker or queue loop.
